The point is NOT to ban these outright. A single instance is fine and sometimes
correct. AI writing gives itself away by CLUSTERS of them plus the sentence
patterns below. When editing, hunt for clusters and rewrite; keep a word if it
is the plain, honest choice in context.

## Sentence patterns to avoid (strong AI tells)

- "It's not about X, it's about Y" / "It's not just X. It's Y" / "That's not X, that's Y"
- "Not because X. But because Y" / "Not by doing X, but by doing Y"
- "No X. No Y. Just Z"
- "X. Y. Z." three consecutive one-word sentences for emphasis
- "And the X? Y" / "The result?" / "The goal?" / "The bottom line?"
- The same two-word phrase repeated two or three times in a row
- Em-dashes used as the default connector; prefer a period, comma, or colon
- Overdone metaphors and analogies (marketing as a playground, chess match, fight)
- Unrealistic exaggerations that weaken credibility

## Corporate filler to avoid

continuous improvement, solution development, strategic alignment,
operational excellence, organizational efficiency

## How to sound human instead

- Write like you talk. Read it aloud; if you would not say it, cut it.
- Use specifics: real numbers, names, examples, lived detail, not safe,
  statistically-likely phrasing.
- Do not over-correct. Swapping a flagged word for a rare thesaurus synonym
  reads MORE like AI (the over-correction tell), not less. Plain words win.
- Judge in clusters, not singles: one flagged word does not mean it is AI; a
  paragraph stacked with them does. Rewrite the stack, not every instance.
- Guide with direction, not bans: prefer "say what actually happened" over a
  blocklist mindset.
